## Overview

Chain Lightning is a lightning spell focused on directed clearing. It jumps between enemies, making it ideal for players who want the speed of lightning gameplay without relying entirely on terrain or random projectile paths. When packs are close together, Chain Lightning can delete groups with very little aiming and keep shock pressure moving across the screen.

The skill sits between [Arc](/guides/skills/arc) and [Spark](/guides/skills/spark). Arc is usually the most automatic chaining spell, Spark fills space with projectiles, and Chain Lightning gives a more deliberate chain-based option that rewards target selection and enemy density. It is a natural page for players comparing lightning spells because it explains the tradeoff between control, coverage, and boss scaling.

Chain Lightning is strongest when there are enough enemies to continue the chain. Against isolated bosses, it needs careful support choices and strong single-target scaling to avoid feeling like a clear-only tool. This is why many builds pair it with another lightning skill or adjust supports for boss encounters.

## Mechanics

Chain Lightning scales through lightning damage, spell damage, cast speed, chain behavior, critical strike investment, shock effect, and lightning penetration. Its defining mechanic is that damage moves from target to target. The more enemies available, the more natural the skill feels. If enemies are spread too far apart, the chain value drops.

Target selection matters. Starting the chain on a central enemy or one near the densest part of the pack usually produces better clear than hitting an isolated edge target. In boss fights with adds, Chain Lightning can gain value by bouncing through supporting enemies. In pure single-target fights, it needs support from penetration, stronger hits, or a complementary skill.

Shock is a major benefit because repeated lightning hits can improve follow-up damage. This creates natural synergy with [Ball Lightning](/guides/skills/ball-lightning), [Spark](/guides/skills/spark), [Arc](/guides/skills/arc), and lightning attack pages such as [Lightning Arrow](/skills/lightning-arrow).

## Best Support Gems

Lightning Penetration is a core support for keeping damage relevant against tougher enemies. Faster Casting improves clear speed and makes chain application feel smoother. Added Lightning Damage or high spell damage supports help early and midgame damage. Critical supports become valuable if your passive tree and gear already support critical strikes.

Chain-oriented supports are only valuable if they solve a real problem. If the skill already clears packs well, extra chain behavior may be less important than boss damage. If the skill feels awkward in spread packs, better chaining or coverage may be exactly what it needs.

For bossing, lean toward damage, penetration, and uptime. For mapping, lean toward cast speed and chain reliability. The strongest setup is the one that matches the content you are actually running.

## Strengths

Chain Lightning's greatest strength is efficient pack clear with low aiming friction. It can move through groups quickly and apply lightning damage to enemies that are not directly under your cursor. This makes it comfortable in dense maps and campaign zones.

It also has excellent internal linking value. A reader can branch into [Arc](/guides/skills/arc), [Spark](/guides/skills/spark), [Ball Lightning](/guides/skills/ball-lightning), [Lightning Arrow](/guides/skills/lightning-arrow), [Lightning Ranger](/builds/lightning-ranger), and boss pages such as [Executioner](/bosses/executioner). That makes Chain Lightning part of a strong lightning authority cluster.

Another strength is feedback. You can immediately see whether enemies are close enough for the skill to work well. This makes it easier to learn than skills whose damage pattern is less visible.

## Weaknesses

Chain Lightning is density-dependent. If there are too few enemies, the chain mechanic loses value. This can make isolated bosses feel slower unless the build has enough single-target investment.

The skill can also encourage careless positioning. Because it clears packs from range, players may delay defensive upgrades or stand still too long while casting. In harder content, that habit becomes dangerous.

Finally, Chain Lightning can compete with other lightning spells for the same build slot. If you want maximum automatic targeting, [Arc](/guides/skills/arc) may feel better. If you want terrain-based area pressure, [Spark](/guides/skills/spark) or [Ball Lightning](/guides/skills/ball-lightning) may be more satisfying.

## Leveling

Chain Lightning is strong for leveling because packs are common and the skill reduces aiming pressure. Upgrade wands or staves frequently, take efficient lightning and spell nodes, and add cast speed when mana sustain allows.

During the campaign, aim at central enemies rather than isolated targets. If a rare monster survives after the pack dies, use a higher damage support or a secondary single-target tool. Do not let smooth pack clear hide weak gear; bosses will reveal those gaps quickly.

Leveling defenses matter too. Chain Lightning keeps you at range, but ranged enemies, ground effects, and boss lunges still threaten caster builds. Add life, resistances, and recovery as you progress.

## Endgame

In endgame mapping, Chain Lightning works best in dense layouts where enemies stay close enough for chains to continue. Fast cast speed, lightning penetration, and reliable shock make it feel responsive. It can also pair with [Lightning Ranger](/builds/lightning-ranger) cluster content for readers exploring lightning themes across attacks and spells.

Bossing requires more planning. Against [Count Geonor](/bosses/count-geonor), [Executioner](/bosses/executioner), or [Endgame Titan](/bosses/endgame-titan), identify safe casting windows and use support choices that improve single-target damage. If the encounter has few or no adds, do not expect the chain mechanic to carry the fight alone.

The best endgame Chain Lightning builds treat it as a fast clear tool with enough boss support to avoid a hard wall.

Another endgame consideration is how the skill behaves when map density changes. Chain Lightning can feel effortless in crowded areas and then suddenly ordinary in sparse rooms. Strong builds prepare for both cases by keeping a boss-ready support plan available and by maintaining enough raw lightning damage that the first hit matters even before the chain begins. This keeps the skill from becoming dependent on perfect monster spacing.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is overbuilding for clear when clear is already solved. Once packs die quickly, invest in penetration, defenses, or boss damage. Another mistake is aiming at edge enemies and wasting chain potential.

Players also sometimes ignore mana sustain. Faster casting is valuable, but only when the build can keep casting during real fights.

## FAQ

### Is Chain Lightning good for leveling?

Yes. It is comfortable for campaign clearing because it jumps between enemies and reduces the need for precise aiming.

### Why is boss damage weaker than pack clear?

The chain mechanic gains value from multiple targets. Against one enemy, you need penetration, stronger hits, and better uptime to compensate.

### What stats should I prioritize?

Lightning damage, spell damage, cast speed, lightning penetration, shock effect, and critical scaling if your build supports it.

### Is Chain Lightning the same as Arc?

No. They share a chaining identity, but [Arc](/guides/skills/arc) is generally more automatic while Chain Lightning emphasizes directed chaining and pack density.

### Which skills pair well with Chain Lightning?

[Ball Lightning](/guides/skills/ball-lightning), [Spark](/guides/skills/spark), [Arc](/guides/skills/arc), and [Ice Spear](/guides/skills/ice-spear) can all cover different combat needs.

### Can Chain Lightning be an endgame main skill?

Yes, if the build solves single-target damage, mana sustain, and defenses instead of relying only on pack-clearing strength.
